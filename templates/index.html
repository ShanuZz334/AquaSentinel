System Pipeline Topology

Stage 1

Optical Physics Correction

Compensates for wavelength-dependent attenuation (Beer-Lambert law) and applies adaptive histogram equalization to recover contrast lost to backscattering.

Stage 2

Spatial Feature Grounding

Open-vocabulary Vision Transformer (ViT) projects regional patch tokens to detect spatial coordinates and bounding anchors across marine substrates.

Stage 3

Linguistic Reasoning

Autoregressive Language Model conditions on detected coordinates and user directives to synthesize structured situational analyses and risk indices.

Stage 4

Operational Dispatch

Outputs formatted telemetry, severity ratings, and actionable navigational protocols for underwater exploration units.

Technical Review & Defense Points

How does the system distinguish Computer Vision from Natural Language Processing?

Computer Vision is responsible for optical attenuation compensation, image tensor normalization, and visual feature extraction via Vision Transformers to output pixel bounding coordinates. Natural Language Processing parses open-vocabulary mission directives and performs autoregressive linguistic decoding to generate structured ecological assessments.

Why do terrestrial vision-language models degrade in underwater environments?

Terrestrial models are trained predominantly on natural light scenes where red, green, and blue wavelengths travel uniformly. In water, selective red-light absorption, backscattering, and suspended particulate degrade contrast, causing standard terrestrial models to misclassify benthic substrate or miss hazards.

What distinguishes this from traditional bounding-box detectors (e.g., YOLO)?

Traditional object detectors are restricted to a closed class taxonomy. They cannot interpret complex conversational questions, determine whether an object constitutes an entanglement risk, or evaluate ecological consequences. AquaSentinel provides open-vocabulary grounding combined with contextual reasoning.
